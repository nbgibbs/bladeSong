### **Mill Hollow**  
- **Location**: A small hamlet, once the center of the region’s milling industry, located just up the river from Cardend. The hamlet was nestled in a narrow valley at the mouth of a canyon, making it naturally defensible. Mill Hollow's most distinguishing feature was the watermill that powered the community's grain processing — a lifeline for the families living there. The small fortification, a motte and bailey structure, was built atop a rise above the mill, offering a good view of the surrounding area.

- **Population**: At its height, Mill Hollow had about 100 people, with the village centered around the mill and its surrounding homes. After the landslide that dammed the river, the population dwindled, and the once-bustling hamlet was slowly abandoned. Most of the villagers relocated, either to Cardend or other nearby towns. 

- **The Landslide**: About 50 years ago, a massive landslide tore through the valley, damming the river just before it reached the mill. The rising water quickly flooded the lower half of the hamlet, including all the homes and most of the businesses, leaving only the upper parts of the motte and bailey above the waterline. With the floodwater isolating the people, and the mill no longer functional, the hamlet’s fortifications lost their strategic importance. Within a year, nearly everyone had left, abandoning the mill behind.

- **The Mill's Return**: After a year, the river slowly began to erode the dam, and the mill started working again, though in a reduced capacity. Now, it is a lonely, failing structure with little hope of ever returning to its former glory. The mill’s ruins are a haunting reminder of what was lost when the landslide struck.
